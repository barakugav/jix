# F019: f64 jix.log(x, base) for a base other than e/2/10 multiplies ln(x) by a rounded reciprocal of ln(base): exact powers come out 1 ulp off (log(64, base=4) = 2.9999999999999996), and power-of-two bases are inexact

- Severity: low  Verdict: confirmed  Category: questionable-semantics (precision)
- Location: jix-py/src/ops/op1.rs:366 (related: jix-py/src/ops/op1.rs:370, jix-py/python/tests/test_op1.py:139, bug-hunt/KNOWN.md K92, bug-hunt/runs/2026-10-27-batch5/findings/F008.md)
- Reachability: Python API (the Rust core has no log-with-base op; the wrapper builds it from `Cast` + `Ln` + `map`)
- Reported by: py-ops-arith-wrappers

## What goes wrong
The #92 fix for K92 routes bases e / 2 / 10 to the dedicated `Ln` / `Log2` / `Log10` kernels and, for
any other base, computes in f64 and casts once. That fixes f16 / f32 (the result is rounded to the narrow
type once and comes out exact on the cases tried). For f64 output, though, it still computes
`ln(x) * (1.0 / ln(base))`: four roundings (ln(base), the reciprocal, ln(x), the product) instead of the
three of `ln(x) / ln(base)` (what `math.log(x, b)` and `np.log(x) / np.log(b)` compute). F008's suggested
fix for this case ("f64: at least divide by ln(base) instead of multiplying by its rounded reciprocal,
or use log2(x)/log2(base)") was not applied. Exact powers of the base land 1 ulp off,
e.g. `jix.log(64.0, base=4) = 2.9999999999999996`, so `int(...)` / `floor(...)` give 2. With power-of-two
bases (4, 8, 16, 32, 0.5, 0.25), where `log2(x) / log2(base)` would be exact on every power, 9 to 18
powers per base are wrong. On random inputs, 8% of results have more than 1 ulp of error,
against 0.1-0.5% for `ln/ln`. The maximum error stays below 2 ulp, so this is a precision defect, not a
large wrong result.

## Trigger
```python
jix.log(np.array([64.0]), base=4)        # 2.9999999999999996 (math.log, np.log/np.log, f32 jix: 3.0)
jix.log(np.array([4.0**-7]), base=4)     # -6.999999999999999
jix.log(np.array([4096.0]), base=16)     # 2.9999999999999996
jix.log(np.array([0.125]), base=0.5)     # 2.9999999999999996
int(jix.log(np.array([64.0]), base=4).numpy()[0])   # 2
```

"Exact powers" are the k in [-40, 40] where base**k is exactly representable. The random inputs are
exp(U(-50, 50)), measured against a 50-digit `decimal` reference. Neither method is correctly
rounded, and `ln/ln` is not exact on every power either (base 8: 18, base 3: 12). The exact-power claim
that holds in general is the power-of-two one: `log2(x) / log2(base)` is exact on all of them. The
general claim is about the error distribution: multiplying by a rounded reciprocal puts about 15x more
results above 1 ulp than dividing does.

## Root cause
jix-py/src/ops/op1.rs:366 precomputes `ln_base_inv = 1.0 / base.ln()`, which adds a rounding (up to
0.5 ulp of relative error in the multiplier). op1.rs:370 then rounds `x * ln_base_inv` once more. For
f16 / f32 the f64 error is absorbed by the final cast to T. For f64 it is the final result. The test at
jix-py/python/tests/test_op1.py:139 checks a general base with `rtol=1e-3`, which cannot see a 1-2 ulp
error, and never checks exact powers with a non-2/10 base. The context doc (py-ops.md, "Changed in df2f918")
describes the general-base path as "computed in f64 and cast once (K92)". That describes the f16 / f32 fix
and does not accept the f64 behavior. Nothing in KNOWN.md or the "Intentional behaviors" lists covers it.

## Suggested fix
In the general-base arm, divide instead of multiplying by the reciprocal: capture `ln_base = base.ln()`
and map `x / ln_base`. For a power-of-two base (`base.log2()` is an integer, i.e. the mantissa of
`base` is exactly 0.5 after `frexp`), dispatch to `Log2` and divide by `base.log2()`, which is then
exact on every power of the base. Add a test asserting exact f64 results for, e.g., `log(64, base=4)`,
`log(4096, base=16)`, `log(0.125, base=0.5)`.
